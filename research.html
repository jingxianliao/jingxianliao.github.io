Research

Aerodynamics and actuator effectiveness change as convertible aircraft transition between hover and forward flight. My work addresses these challenges through aerodynamic modeling and adaptive control allocation.

AeroKAN

Aerodynamic modeling

AeroKAN combines an analytical lift prior with a learned residual in the edge functions of a Kolmogorov–Arnold network to capture nonlinear aerodynamic behavior.

Symmetric airfoils
24.8% lower MAE
AeroKAN achieved a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.0246 across 57 configurations for which the physical prior is valid. The baseline was a KAN without the prior.
Cambered airfoils
20.8% lower MAE
Across 23 configurations for 11 airfoils, MAE averaged 0.0566 for models that encode the prior in their edge functions and 0.0715 for models that impose it through a loss penalty.

allocKAN

Adaptive control

allocKAN adapts control allocation online as actuator effectiveness changes. A composite Lyapunov analysis establishes conditions for uniform ultimate boundedness of the closed-loop system.

Compared with allocMLP
37.9 s vs 75.7 s
Mean recovery time after abrupt faults across six closed-loop simulations. In offline comparisons at similar parameter counts, allocKAN achieved 75.2% lower validation mean squared error than allocMLP on average.
Compared with conventional allocators
Bounded closed-loop states
All five conventional allocators diverged in simulations with dual actuator reversal, both with and without additional wind disturbances and inertia uncertainty.

Current research

Convertible aircraft

I develop gray-box models of tiltrotor transition dynamics by combining physics-based models with data. The ongoing project includes plans for flight validation.

Embodied intelligence

I investigate compact models for robot dynamics and control, starting with edge-function selection in KANs under a model-size budget and prediction of robotic manipulator end-effector positions.
